The Fabric Guide

Fabric decides how a piece feels, how it moves and how long it lasts. Learn the main fibres and you will shop, wear and wash with far more confidence.

Natural fibres

FibreFeelsGood forCare
CottonSoft, breathableShirts, tees, denimMachine wash; may shrink hot
LinenCrisp, cool, creasesSummer shirts, trousersWash cool; iron damp
WoolWarm, springyKnits, suits, coatsCool wool cycle; dry flat
SilkSmooth, fluidBlouses, scarvesHand wash or dry clean

Synthetic and regenerated fibres

  • Polyester: strong, quick-drying and crease-resistant, but less breathable.
  • Nylon: very strong; used in outerwear and hosiery.
  • Elastane: adds stretch; a little goes a long way.
  • Viscose and lyocell: made from wood pulp; soft and drapey, often cooler than polyester.

Weave and knit

The same fibre can feel completely different depending on how it is made. Woven fabrics such as poplin, twill and denim are stable and structured. Knitted fabrics such as jersey and rib stretch and recover, which is why T-shirts and jumpers are knits.

Judging quality in the shop

  1. Hold it to the light: an even, dense weave lasts longer.
  2. Scrunch it for five seconds: good fabric recovers with few creases.
  3. Check the seams: straight, tight stitching with no pulling.
  4. Feel the inside: rough or scratchy linings rarely improve.

Choosing for the weather

In heat, choose loose weaves and natural or regenerated fibres such as linen, cotton and lyocell. In cold, wool and wool blends trap warm air even when slightly damp. The Fabric Finder suggests a starting point.

Making fabrics last

Wash less often and at lower temperatures, turn garments inside out, air-dry where you can and fold knits rather than hanging them. Most wear comes from washing and drying, not from wearing.
