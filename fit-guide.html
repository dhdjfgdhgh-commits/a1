The Fit Guide

Good fit is the quickest way to look put together. Here is how the main pieces should fit, and what a tailor can and cannot change.

Take your measurements

  • Chest or bust: around the fullest part, tape level.
  • Waist: around your natural waist, the narrowest part.
  • Hips: around the fullest part of the seat.
  • Inseam: from the crotch to the ankle on the inside of the leg.

Compare these with each brand’s size chart rather than relying on the size label.

Shirts and tops

Shoulder seams sit on the edge of the shoulder bone. You should be able to button the shirt without the fabric pulling into an X across the chest. Sleeves end at the wrist bone with arms relaxed.

Trousers and jeans

The waist should sit without a belt doing the work. The rise (from crotch to waistband) should feel comfortable when sitting. Hems should just touch the top of the shoe for straight cuts, or show the ankle for cropped styles.

Jackets and coats

Shoulders should be smooth with no dents below the seam. With the jacket buttoned, you should be able to slide a flat hand inside. A coat needs room for a jumper underneath without pulling.

What a tailor can fix

EasyPossibleHard or not worth it
Hemming trousers and skirtsTaking in a jacket’s sidesShoulders that are too wide
Taking in a waistShortening jacket sleevesLengthening anything with little seam allowance
Shortening shirt sleevesTapering trouser legsA jacket that is too short

Sizes across borders

US, UK and EU sizes use different numbers, and letter sizes vary by brand. The Clothing Size Converter gives a quick conversion; always check the measurements before you buy.
